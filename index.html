The Mission

The People's Department sustains the talent of a new generation

To meet the challenges of an ever-evolving, technology-driven security landscape, USDA is investing in the engineers, analysts, and builders who will define how the federal government delivers for the American people — in food, nutrition, and beyond. We are looking for the next 10 years of technological strategy.

One Farmer, One File

Unify a producer's data across dozens of disconnected USDA systems so a farmer enters their information once — not once per agency, per program, per visit.

Geospatial & Drone Research

Build the data pipelines and imagery tooling behind ARS field science — from drone-captured crop imagery to the geospatial models that turn it into research-grade signal.

Modernizing Legacy Delivery

Replace decades-old systems that touch SNAP, WIC, and farm loan delivery — the software that decides whether benefits and credit reach people on time.

Conservation Decision Support

Build the tools foresters and conservationists use to plan treatments, model outcomes, and steward 193 million acres of public forests and grasslands.

Animal Health Surveillance

Stand up the platforms that track and respond to animal-disease outbreaks nationwide — surveillance data that protects livestock, supply chains, and public health.

Securing Critical Infrastructure

Defend the food and agriculture sector as critical infrastructure: zero-trust rollout across 100k+ endpoints and threat hunting against adversaries who treat agriculture as a strategic target.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers

Everything you need to know about applying, clearance, compensation, and what working at USDA actually looks like day-to-day.

How do I apply if I'm not a U.S. citizen? +

Eligibility is generally restricted to U.S. citizens — plan on citizenship being required for permanent positions. A small number of roles may be open to U.S. nationals or lawful permanent residents where law specifically allows, but those are the exception, not the rule.

Can I work remotely? +

Most teams are primarily in-office at one of seven tech hubs — typically 4–5 days a week on-site. We don't pretend to be remote-first; there's real value in being close to the program teams and the people who use what you build. The exact telework posture varies by role and is stated in each posting. Relocation assistance is available for many roles.

What's the security clearance requirement? +

Most roles require a Public Trust or Secret-level background investigation. We sponsor it — you don't need a clearance to apply. Some cyber and infrastructure roles require Top Secret; we'll fund and shepherd that process. Plan for roughly 2–6 months from offer to start.

What job series should I search for? +

The most common is IT-2210 (information technology). You'll also see 1550 (computer science) and 0854 (computer engineering). Data, geospatial, and design roles can appear under series like 1560, 0150, and 1084. When in doubt, start with 2210 and broaden from there.

Can military veterans get hiring preference for tech roles? +

Yes. Veterans' preference and special hiring authorities — VEOA, VRA, and 30% disabled veteran appointments — apply to technology roles just like any other. See our Veterans Employment page for the details.

I'm a current federal employee — how do I transfer in? +

Apply to merit promotion listings on USAJobs as a status candidate. A lateral move at the same grade generally doesn't require a new competitive process. Search the merit-promotion listings filtered to USDA and the 2210 series.

What does the interview process look like? +

Application and résumé review (about 2 weeks) → technical screen → panel interview → references and clearance kickoff. Total is roughly 8–14 weeks from apply to start, longer if your role needs higher clearance. We tell you what stage you're at, every step — no ghosting.

Do you sponsor visas? +

No. USDA does not sponsor employment visas for these positions, and U.S. citizenship is generally required.

How does federal pay actually compare? +

Honestly: base salaries typically run 70–85% of FAANG total comp at the same level. We won't pretend otherwise. What changes the math is the rest of the package — pension plus TSP match, healthcare into retirement, 11 holidays, and real 40-hour weeks — and the fact that several hubs (Kansas City, Fort Collins, Indianapolis) sit well below the national cost-of-living index. The case here is impact, scope, and stability, not the top-of-band number.
